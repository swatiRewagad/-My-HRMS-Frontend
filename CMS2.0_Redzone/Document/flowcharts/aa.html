Appellate Authority — appeals against an Ombudsman decision

Who may appeal, within what time, and how the appeal is heard and decided

Written for business readers · every step below is what the software does today Prepared from the CMS 2.0 source code, 30 Sep 2026

In one paragraph

Once an Ombudsman closes a complaint, the losing side may appeal. The system is strict about who and when: the complaint must already be closed, the closure must have been made under a clause that the Scheme allows an appeal against, and who you are decides what you may appeal — a citizen has broader grounds than a bank. Inside 30 days the appeal is filed freely; between 31 and 60 days a written reason for the delay is required; after 60 days it is refused. An accepted appeal is registered, allocated to a bench, a hearing is scheduled, and the Appellate Authority passes a final order. The order can uphold the Ombudsman, modify the decision, set it aside, dismiss the appeal, or send the case back to the Ombudsman to look at again.

1. Who does what

RoleWhat this person does in the system
Registrar's officeReceives the appeal. Accepts or rejects it on admission, allocates it to a bench, and asks the parties for documents.
Bench officerPrepares the case brief, schedules the hearing, escalates to a higher bench, or sends the file back to the Registrar.
Secretariat of the Appellate AuthoritySchedules hearings, and issues the final order — upholding, modifying, setting aside, dismissing, or remanding.
AdministratorReassign, close, or reopen an appeal.
AppellantEither the citizen, or the bank's Principal Nodal Officer. Files the appeal and attends the hearing.

2. Can this appeal even be filed?

Five gates, in this order. Failing any one stops the appeal.

  1. Does the complaint exist?
  2. Is it actually finished? It must be closed, resolved, rejected, adjudicated or conciliated. A complaint the citizen withdrew is deliberately excluded — you cannot appeal your own withdrawal.
  3. Is there already an appeal running on it? Only one live appeal per complaint.
  4. How long ago was it closed? 0–30 days: file freely · 31–60 days: allowed, but a reason for the delay is mandatory · beyond 60 days: refused
  5. Was it closed on a ground that can be appealed, by you? The closure clause is looked up and matched against who is asking. A citizen may appeal on two grounds; a bank on only one. If the closure clause was never recorded, or is not on the list, no appeal is possible.
This last gate blocks real cases today Thousands of already-closed complaints in the database have no closure clause recorded. For every one of them the appeal cannot be filed — the system refuses with a service error rather than a polite explanation. Worse, the "can I appeal?" pre-check on the screen answers optimistically and the actual filing then fails. This needs a data clean-up, not a code change.
You cannot claim to be the other side Which grounds apply is worked out from the signed-in user's own roles, not from anything sent by the browser. A bank user cannot file an appeal pretending to be the complainant.

4. Clocks and deadlines

30 daysto appeal freely after the complaint is closed
31–60 daysappeal still allowed, but a written reason for the delay is required
Over 60 daysappeal refused outright
7 daysexpected minimum notice before a hearing
7 daysinternal target to admit a newly filed appeal
30 daysinternal target once under review, and again once a hearing is fixed
Two caveats on these numbers 1. The 7-day hearing notice is advisory. The system warns but does not refuse a shorter-notice hearing.
2. The internal targets (7 / 30 / 30 working days) are flagged in the code itself as operational estimates, not figures taken from the Scheme. They should be confirmed before being quoted externally.

5. How appeals are allocated to officers

Allocation is automatic and follows a strict order:

  1. Reserve the rotation marker so two appeals arriving at the same moment cannot be given to the same officer.
  2. Find the pool of eligible officers. If nobody is eligible the appeal is left deliberately unassigned — the system never invents an assignee.
  3. Language override. If the appeal is in a regional language and an officer speaks it, they get it regardless of workload, and the rotation marker is not advanced.
  4. Remove officers who are at capacity.
  5. Pick the next in rotation, breaking ties by who has the lightest chargeable workload.
  6. If everyone is at capacity the appeal is left unassigned and flagged. There is a configurable allowance to exceed capacity in an emergency, but it is switched off by default.
  7. Notify the officer only after the allocation is safely saved — never before.
Safe under load Two servers cannot assign the same appeal twice — the database itself enforces uniqueness, and the second attempt is rejected cleanly rather than creating a duplicate.

6. Things to know before relying on this

This is the best-enforced module in the system Unlike the other workflow modules, the Appellate Authority genuinely checks both the caller's role and the appeal's current state on every action. A wrong-role attempt is properly refused, and an out-of-order attempt is refused with a list of the actions that are valid right now. The list of available actions shown on screen is read from the same single table that enforces them, so the screen and the server cannot drift apart.
What you might assumeWhat the code actually does
"An appeal can be saved as a draft first" There is no draft state. An appeal either exists as filed, or it does not exist.
"A rejected or closed appeal is truly final" An administrator can reopen any finished appeal. This is the only route out of a final outcome and it is not restricted by which outcome it was.
"Orders are issued as signed PDF documents" Orders are stored and served as text only. No document is generated.
"An order can be corrected" Yes, but only as a new revision — the original is never overwritten. Attempting to issue a second original order is refused.
"Notices are sent to the citizen" They are not. The system records an obligation to notify, and nothing in the code ever marks a notice as actually sent. Dispatch is not built. Every notice sits permanently as pending.
"Award limits and the court-case block are enforced" Yes — both are active by default. An award above the Scheme cap is refused, and an appeal on a matter already before a court is blocked.
"A remand just informs the Ombudsman" It genuinely reopens the original complaint: back to in-progress, marked as remanded, and its closure date and clause are wiped. If the original complaint cannot be found the remand fails outright rather than silently proceeding.
"A CEPC case can be appealed here" No. Nothing in the consumer protection cell module leads to an appeal. Only Ombudsman closures are appealable.
"There is one path to a hearing" There are two — a bench officer and the Secretariat can each schedule one, and they are not reconciled with each other.

Refusal shapes

Role mismatch ⇒ 403 AaAccessDeniedException. From-status mismatch ⇒ 409 AaIllegalTransitionException, body carries availableActions.

Filing gates

complaint exists → status ∈ {closed, resolved, rejected, adjudicated, conciliated} (withdrawn deliberately excluded) → no active appeal → days since closure: 0–30 eligible / 31–60 delayedFiling with mandatory reason / >60 ineligible → appealability from the closure clause.

isAppealableBy(party): complainant ⇒ 15(1)(a) + 15(1)(b); entity ⇒ 15(1)(b) only. Null, blank or unmapped clause ⇒ not appealable. The party is derived from the caller's own roles.

Check path fails OPEN, filing path fails CLOSED. The pre-check returns 200 {appealable:false}, but filing raises UnmappedClauseException from AppealClassificationService.classify, rethrown at AppealController:182 as a 503. Clause-less closed complaints therefore cannot be appealed at all.

Assignment engine, in order

  1. Lock the pointer row — ensureExists in its own committed transaction to avoid an InnoDB gap-lock self-deadlock, then findByRoleGroupForUpdate.
  2. Build the pool. Empty ⇒ UNASSIGNED_POOL_EMPTY; never fabricate an assignee.
  3. Vernacular override — threshold-exempt, and the pointer is not advanced.
  4. Capacity filter.
  5. Rotation sorted by (chargeableWorkload, userId). The pointer stores a user id, not an index.
  6. Exhaustion ⇒ UNASSIGNED_POOL_EXHAUSTED. assignment.grace_allowance defaults to 0 = disabled.
  7. place() — a DB unique index is the multi-pod lock; violation ⇒ 409. Notification is sent after commit only.

Hearings

SCHEDULED → (RESCHEDULED* | ADJOURNED) → COMPLETED. Append-only; the service chooses the event type, not the caller; supersede chain maintained. min_notice_days = 7 is advisory only.

Orders

Append-only. Revision 1, then correct creates N+1. A second original issue ⇒ 409. Outcomes: UPHELD, MODIFIED, SET_ASIDE, REMANDED, DISMISSED. Text only — no PDF generation.

Remand side-effects on the parent complaint

status → in_progress; stage → REMANDED_BY_AA; closedAt and closureClause nulled; reopenCount++. A missing parent hard-fails.

Notices

Created as PENDING obligations. No code path sets SENT. Idempotency lock is the unique key (appealNumber, eventCode, recipientRole, dedupeKey).

Configuration

Armed by default: award cap ₹33,00,000 (₹30L + ₹3L) and the sub-judice block.
Off by default: require_ed_approval, hearing.reminder_enabled, assignment.grace_allowance, auto_rebalance.

SLA

filed 7 / under_review 30 / hearing_scheduled 30 working days — explicitly flagged in the code as operational guesses, not Scheme figures.

Open interface gap

SCHEDULE_HEARING is granted to both AA_REVIEWER and AA_SECRETARIAT, producing two hearing-creation paths that are not reconciled.
