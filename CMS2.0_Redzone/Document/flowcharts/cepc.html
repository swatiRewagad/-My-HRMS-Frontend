CEPC — Consumer Education & Protection Cell

The regional-office review chain for grievances the Ombudsman scheme does not cover

Written for business readers · every step below is what the software does today Prepared from the CMS 2.0 source code, 30 Sep 2026

In one paragraph

Grievances that fall outside the Ombudsman scheme — or that concern institutions the scheme does not reach — go to a regional Consumer Education & Protection Cell instead. A Dealing Official accepts the case and gathers information: asking the complainant for more detail, consulting another RBI department, or routing it to a designated contact person at the institution. A meeting can be convened. When the file is ready it goes up a four-person review chain — Dealing Official → Reviewer → In-charge → Closing Authority — and each level can send it back down. The Closing Authority either closes the case with a letter, or forwards it out: to another RBI office, to another RBI department, or to a different regulator altogether.

1. Who does what

RoleWhat this person does in the system
Dealing OfficialAccepts the case, requests information, consults other departments, forwards to a contact person at the institution, arranges meetings, and submits the file for review.
ReviewerQuality-checks the file. Approves it upward to the In-charge, or sends it back to the Dealing Official.
In-chargeSecond level of review. Approves for closure, or sends it back to the Reviewer. Can also reassign a case to a different officer.
Closing AuthorityFinal say. Closes the case with a letter, or forwards it out of CEPC entirely.
Contact PersonA designated person at the institution. Responds to the query, or asks for it to be passed to a colleague.
AdministratorEscalate, close, reopen, and reassign.

3. The same journey as a checklist

  1. Dealing Official Accept the case. This is the only point at which a target date is set.
  2. Dealing Official Gather what is needed. Ask the complainant for more information, consult another RBI department, or route it to the institution's contact person. Any of these can be repeated.
  3. Dealing Official Convene a meeting if required. Scheduling a meeting does not move the case — it is recorded against it.
  4. Dealing Official Submit the file. Either to the Reviewer, or directly to the In-charge if the Reviewer step is being skipped.
  5. Reviewer → In-charge Approve upward or send back. A Reviewer sends back to the Dealing Official; an In-charge sends back to the Reviewer.
  6. Closing Authority Close it, or forward it out. Closing records a reason and generates the closure letter automatically. Forwarding out requires the destination to be named — this is the only place the system actually refuses incomplete input.

4. Clocks and deadlines

7 daystarget for a critical case
15 daystarget for a high-priority case
30 daystarget for a normal case — also the default
45 daystarget for a low-priority case

Counted in working days only — Monday to Friday, 9:00 am to 6:00 pm, Indian time, with declared holidays excluded.

Important limitation CEPC has no deadline monitoring at all. There is no background job. Whether a case is overdue is worked out only when someone opens the statistics screen, which sorts cases into breached, at-risk (two days or fewer remaining) and on-track. Nobody is notified when a deadline passes, and no case escalates on its own. The target date is also only ever set at three moments: acceptance, reopening, and manual creation — a case that arrives and is never accepted has no deadline whatsoever.

5. Things to know before relying on this

The biggest one — CEPC enforces almost nothing The application decides what buttons to show based on the user's role and the case's current state. The server does not re-check either. The checking logic exists and is correct, but it is only consulted by the read-only screens that ask "what can I do here?" — it is never consulted when the action is actually performed. The practical effect: any of the six CEPC roles can perform any CEPC action on any case, from any state. A Dealing Official can close a case; a contact person can approve a review.
What you might assumeWhat the code actually does
"Only the Closing Authority can close a case" That is what the screens show, but the server accepts a close from any CEPC role.
"A case must pass through the Reviewer" The Dealing Official can legitimately skip straight to the In-charge, and in practice the order is not enforced at all.
"The conciliation screen works" It does not, on three counts. It is not linked into the application at all, so nobody can reach it. The three actions it sends are not CEPC actions, so they fall through to unrelated logic borrowed from the Ombudsman module. Two of them assign the case to a role that does not exist, effectively orphaning it. The third simply returns "unknown action".
"Uploading a document to a CEPC case saves it" The upload is only held in the browser. It is never sent to the server and is lost on refresh.
"The history and escalations screens show different things" Both menu items load the same screen. No separate escalations view exists.
"The citizen can see progress through the review chain" No. None of the CEPC states — information requested, referred to a department, with the Reviewer, with the In-charge, awaiting closure, forwarded out — is recognised by the citizen tracking page. Every one of them displays as "Stage 1 · Registered". A CEPC complainant sees no movement at all until the case is closed.
"A CEPC case can be escalated to the Appellate Authority" There is no such action. Nothing in the CEPC module references appeals. The "escalate" action only stamps a label on the case — it does not change the department or reassign it to anyone.
"Cases always reach the right cell" When the institution cannot be identified, routing defaults to CEPC. Unidentified complaints therefore accumulate here.
Technical appendix — for developers and testers

The enforcement gap, precisely

CepcWorkflowService.performAction (line 123) calls executeAction directly. It never calls validateRoleAuthorization (line 266) nor isActionValidForState (line 588). Those two are reachable only from GET …/validate-action and GET …/available-actions/{n}. The only runtime check is the coarse class-level list on @CepcRoleGuard (WorkflowController.java:174), aspect security/CepcRoleGuardAspect.java — JWT-first, X-User-Roles honoured only when cms.security.allow-dev-identity-headers=true.

Status vocabulary

Lifecycle: assigned → in_progress → (info_requested | forwarded | forwarded_to_contact) → reviewer_review → incharge_review → awaiting_closure → closed. Side states: sent_back, escalated, forwarded_external, and sent_to_other (written by InterOfficeTransferService, not by CEPC).

ROLE_ACTIONS, lines 78–100. Ladder CEPC_DO → CEPC_REVIEWER → CEPC_INCHARGE → CEPC_CLOSING_AUTHORITY; off-ladder CEPC_ADMIN, CEPC_CONTACT_PERSON. Dead role: CEPC_OFFICER — in no guard and no ROLE_ACTIONS entry, yet still emitted by ComplaintRoutingService:129,144. Non-existent role: CEPC_ADJUDICATOR, only ever set by the unrouted conciliation component's fall-through. Downstream assignee picked by round-robin assignByRole (line 649) via Keycloak, filtering out SECRETARY.
