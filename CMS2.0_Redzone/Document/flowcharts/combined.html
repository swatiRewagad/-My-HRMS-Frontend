End-to-end — a complaint's full journey

How the six parts of the system hand work to each other, from the citizen's first click to the final appeal order

Written for business readers · every step below is what the software does today Prepared from the CMS 2.0 source code, 30 Sep 2026

In one paragraph

A grievance enters the system in one of two ways: the citizen files it on the portal, or it arrives as an email or letter and is typed up at the central intake centre. It is then routed to one of two offices — the Ombudsman office if the Scheme covers it, or the consumer protection cell if not. Both offices work the case up a review ladder, and both can ask the bank for its side of the story through a separate bank portal. Both end in a closure letter. Only an Ombudsman closure can then be taken to the Appellate Authority, which can send the case back to the Ombudsman to be reconsidered.

The pattern to take away Deadlines are strictly enforced at the boundaries — getting into the system, and getting an answer out of the bank. Inside the RBI offices, deadlines are informational only. No case is ever moved, escalated or closed by the system for being late. Overdue work is visible on dashboards but depends entirely on someone acting on it.

4. These are six separate systems, not one workflow

It is important not to read the diagram above as a single pipeline. Each module has its own vocabulary, its own rules and its own degree of enforcement, and they all write to the same complaint record.

5. Cross-module issues worth raising

IssueWhy it matters across modules
Consumer protection cell closures cannot be appealed There is no route from CEPC to the Appellate Authority anywhere in the code. A complainant whose case went to CEPC rather than to an Ombudsman office has no appeal route inside this system.
Many closed complaints have no closure clause recorded Appeal eligibility is decided from the closure clause. Without one, the appeal is refused. Thousands of existing closed complaints are affected — this is a data problem that blocks a whole module.
The citizen view understands only a fraction of the internal states Anything it does not recognise shows as "Registered". The entire consumer protection cell review chain, and several Ombudsman stages, fall into that gap — so citizens see no movement while real work happens, and the stage can also move backwards.
Unidentified institutions default to the consumer protection cell When the system cannot work out which institution is complained about, it sends the case to CEPC. Some of those may belong with an Ombudsman office, and being in CEPC also removes the appeal route.
Refusals that look like successes In both the Ombudsman office and the intake centre, a rejected action frequently returns a normal-looking response with a failure flag inside. Screens that do not read the flag appear to have worked. This affects testing, demos and user trust across two modules.
Notices are recorded but never sent The Appellate Authority records an obligation to notify the parties, but dispatch is not built. Every such notice sits permanently pending.
Nothing inside RBI moves on a timer Only the bank's clock is enforced. Neither Ombudsman nor consumer cell cases escalate, reassign or close on their own. Operational control depends entirely on people watching dashboards.

Routing

ComplaintRoutingService. routeFromPublicPortal — non-RBIO entity ⇒ dept CEPC, role CEPC_DO; UNKNOWN entity fails closed to CEPC. createComplaintFromDraft resolves via routingService.resolveDepartment(entityName), overridden by a targetOffice prefix (RBIO* / CEPC*). routeFromCrpcToApproval (:127) and transferBetweenDepartments (:142) both emit the dead role CEPC_OFFICER.

Inter-office transfer

Both RBIO's FORWARD_TO_OTHER_OFFICE and CEPC's equivalent delegate to InterOfficeTransferService, parking the complaint at sent_to_other. The decision endpoint on CrpcHeadController (APPROVE_TRANSFER / REJECT_TRANSFER) has no role guard.

AA → RBIO remand

REMAND_TO_OMBUDSMAN writes to the parent complaint: status ⇒ in_progress, stage ⇒ REMANDED_BY_AA, closedAt and closureClause nulled, reopenCount++. Missing parent ⇒ hard fail.

RE breach → RBIO ex-parte

ReResponsivenessService.detectBreaches (hardcoded 0 0 22 * * *) sets breached=true and exParteEligible=true on RE_RESPONSE_TRACKER. This is the only automatic cross-module escalation in the entire system, and it still requires an officer to act.

Scheduled jobs across all modules

  • RE — overdue sweep 0 */15 * * * *; nudge 0 0 9 * * *; breach detection 0 0 22 * * *; deadline sweep (externally driven).
  • CRPC — five weekday jobs, all notify-only. ThresholdOverflowService queries a dead status and never fires.
  • RBIO — none. RbioSlaService has no @Scheduled method.
  • CEPC — none. CepcSlaService has no @Scheduled method.
  • AA — hearing.reminder_enabled is off by default.

Appeal eligibility depends on RBIO closure data

AppealClassificationService.classify raises UnmappedClauseException ⇒ 503 at AppealController:182 when closureClause is null, blank or unmapped. isAppealableBy: complainant ⇒ 15(1)(a)+15(1)(b); entity ⇒ 15(1)(b) only. withdrawn is deliberately excluded from appealable statuses. The check endpoint fails open (200 appealable:false); the filing path fails closed (503).
