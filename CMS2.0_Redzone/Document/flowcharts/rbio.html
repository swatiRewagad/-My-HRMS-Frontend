RBIO — Office of the RBI Ombudsman

What happens to a complaint from the day it reaches the Ombudsman's office to the day it is closed

Written for business readers · every step below is what the software does today Prepared from the CMS 2.0 source code, 30 Sep 2026

In one paragraph

A complaint arrives at an Ombudsman office either directly from the citizen portal or handed over by the central intake centre. A Dealing Official picks it up and first decides whether the Ombudsman is even allowed to hear it. If yes, the official gathers facts — usually by asking the bank for a written reply. Once the file is ready the official proposes an outcome and sends it up the ladder: Reviewer → Deputy Ombudsman → Ombudsman. Anyone higher up can push it back down for more work. Most cases end in settlement or conciliation. If the bank will not settle, the case moves to adjudication, where the Ombudsman issues a formal award or rejects the complaint. Either way the citizen gets a closure letter, and may appeal.

1. Who does what

RoleWhat this person does in the system
Dealing OfficialOwns the case day to day. Decides maintainability, asks the bank for its reply, chases documents, records the citizen's rejoinder, drafts the proposed outcome and sends it up for review.
ReviewerQuality-checks the Dealing Official's file. Approves it upward, or sends it back with comments.
Deputy OmbudsmanSecond level of approval. Can approve upward, send back, or take conciliation and adjudication decisions.
OmbudsmanFinal authority. Signs awards, rejects complaints, and approves closures.
Bank / Regulated EntityNot an RBIO user. Receives the complaint on its own portal and files a reply. See the bank's side →
Office AdministratorHousekeeping only — reassign a case to a different officer, or reopen a closed one. Cannot hold a case.
Worth knowing The system carries two sets of role names at the same time — the modern ladder above, and an older set (Officer, Supervisor, Conciliator, Adjudicator) still used by parts of the code. In practice "Conciliator" and "Adjudicator" are not seniority levels; they are stages of the same case that a Deputy Ombudsman or Ombudsman works through.

3. The same journey as a checklist

  1. Dealing Official Open the case. The case moves out of the waiting queue and a target date is set. Citizen sees: "Under review by RBI"
  2. Dealing Official Decide maintainability. If the complaint is outside the Scheme it is closed here and a Scheme clause must be recorded — the system will not let it close without one. If it is maintainable the case simply continues; nothing visible changes for the citizen.
  3. Dealing Official Send it to the bank. The bank gets 15 days. If the bank does not reply, the office can proceed without it. Citizen sees: "With the bank"
  4. Dealing Official Work the file. Chase documents, record the citizen's response to the bank's reply, hold a meeting if needed. Meetings and document requests do not change where the case stands — they are recorded against it.
  5. Dealing Official Propose an outcome and send it up. The case leaves the official's desk and lands with the Reviewer.
  6. Reviewer → Deputy Ombudsman → Ombudsman Approve upward, or send back. A send-back goes to whoever held the case before. Each level must leave a comment when sending back.
  7. Deputy Ombudsman / Ombudsman Close it the right way — settled, conciliated, awarded, or rejected. Every closure needs a reason recorded, and the closure letter is generated from it.
  8. System Tell the citizen. The closure letter is issued; the appeal window opens.

4. Clocks and deadlines

15 daysfor the bank to reply after the complaint is forwarded
30 daysstandard target to dispose of a complaint
60 daystarget once a case enters conciliation
120 daystarget once a case enters adjudication
30 daysappeal window after the closure letter
Important limitation Nothing in RBIO moves on its own. These targets are calculated and displayed, and breaches show up on dashboards and reports — but there is no background job that escalates, reassigns or closes an overdue case. Every single movement of an RBIO case is a person clicking a button. If a case is forgotten, it stays where it is indefinitely.
One exception, and it comes from elsewhere The bank's 15-day clock is genuinely monitored by a nightly job. When it lapses, the case is flagged as eligible to proceed without the bank. Even then an officer still has to act — the flag only unlocks the option. Details on the bank's side →

5. What the citizen actually sees

The citizen's tracking page does not show the internal ladder. Everything collapses into four stages:

Citizen seesWhich internal situations map to it
1 · RegisteredJust filed, not yet picked up
2 · Under review by RBIBeing examined, anywhere on the review ladder, or escalated
3 · With the bankSent to the bank for its reply, or sent back internally
4 · ClosedSettled, conciliated, awarded, rejected, withdrawn or closed
Known gap Several real situations — conciliation in progress, formal notice issued, awaiting documents, transfer pending — are not recognised by the citizen view and silently show as "Registered". A citizen whose case is deep in adjudication can therefore see Stage 1. The citizen stage can also move backwards, for example when a case is sent back internally after having been with the bank.

6. Things to know before relying on this

What you might assumeWhat the code actually does
"Only a Reviewer can approve a review" The role check runs against the case record, and is skipped entirely if the caller's role is not supplied. In that situation the action goes through regardless of who is asking.
"An action is only offered when the case is at the right stage" The screens hide actions that do not apply, but the server does not verify that the case was in a valid state. A case can be driven out of order if the action is invoked directly.
"A rejected action shows an error" Most refusals come back looking like a successful response carrying a failure flag. Screens that do not read that flag will appear to have succeeded. This is the most repeated defect in the module.
"Transferring to another office moves the case" It only requests a transfer. The case parks in a holding state until the central intake head approves or rejects it.
"Send-back always works" If the previous holder is no longer active, the send-back is refused and the officer has to nominate a person explicitly.
"Compensation limits are enforced" Yes — the award amount is checked against the Scheme cap before anything is saved, and an over-cap award is rejected.
Technical appendix — for developers and testers

Five sources decide what an action does — first hit wins

  1. RBIO_WORKFLOW_TRANSITION database rows where isActive='Y'
  2. RbioTransitionRegistry — 24 actions (Wave 0)
  3. RbioLadderActions — 17 actions (Stage 3)
  4. RbioMeetingTransferActions — 5 actions (Stage 5)
  5. effectOf() fallback — resolves by action name alone, ignoring role

Four fields make up "where the case is"

status (legacy lowercase) · workflowStage · milestone · assignedRole + assignedOfficer. A transition row with TO_STATUS = NULL means leave the status unchanged — around ten actions (meetings, document requests, maintainable-decision) rely on this.

Guard order inside applyTransition (line 598)

requireParams → requireComment → uploadLinkGuard → closureCommunicationGuard → implead-incomplete check (only if this is a closure transition) → [nothing persisted yet] → resolveAssignedRole → applySideEffects → write status & stage (both skipped when the effect is APPROVE_LADDER) → milestone → applyAssignee → closureCause → SLA → applyClosureCommunication

The role check is earlier, in performAction (line 248), and is bypassed when userRole is blank.

Role vocabularies

Rank ladder: DEALING_OFFICIAL → REVIEWER → DEPUTY_OMBUDSMAN → OMBUDSMAN. Legacy: OFFICER / SUPERVISOR / CONCILIATOR / ADJUDICATOR — the last two are stages, not ranks. ADMIN is excluded from CASE_HOLDING_ROLES.

Selected action semantics

  • DECIDE_MAINTAINABLE — status unchanged; DECIDE_NON_MAINTAINABLE → closed, requires closureClause.
  • Send-back targets PREVIOUS_HOLDER; no active holder ⇒ refused, client must re-POST with targetUser. HTTP 422 distinguishes "inactive holder" from "no history".
  • CONCILIATION_SUCCESS → conciliated; CONCILIATION_FAILED → status escalated, routed to the adjudication role.
  • Meetings are a status-unchanged loop, blocked by RBIO_STATUS_MASTER.BLOCKS_MEETING, not by from-status.
  • FORWARD_TO_OTHER_OFFICE parks at sent_to_other pending the CRPC Head decision.
  • Legal-case tracking is a side channel with no transition row at all.

SLA

RbioSlaService has no @Scheduled method and performs no transition. Business-day targets: 30 / 30 / 60 / 120.

Refusal shape

IllegalArgumentException is mapped to HTTP 200 with {success:false} — refusals do not reach Angular's error branch.

Citizen projection

CitizenStageMapper: stage 1 = pending,new; 2 = assigned,in_progress,under_review,escalated; 3 = approved,sent_back; 4 = resolved,rejected,closed,withdrawn. Unknown status ⇒ 1. The mapping is not monotonic.
