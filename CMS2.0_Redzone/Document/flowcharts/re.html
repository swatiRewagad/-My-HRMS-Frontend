Bank Portal — the Regulated Entity's side

What a bank's nodal officer sees and does when a complaint is sent to them for a reply

Written for business readers · every step below is what the software does today Prepared from the CMS 2.0 source code, 30 Sep 2026

In one paragraph

When an Ombudsman office wants the bank's version of events, the complaint appears on a separate portal that only that bank can see. A clock starts: 15 days to reply. The bank's nodal officer opens the case, prepares a reply, attaches supporting documents and submits. As they work, the system quietly tracks how far along they are — opened, under review, drafting, documents uploaded, submitted — and shows that progress to the RBI officer. The bank never chooses a status; it is inferred from what they do. If the 15 days lapse, the RBI officer is unlocked to proceed without the bank. Submitting late is still allowed.

1. Who does what

RoleWhat this person does in the system
Nodal OfficerThe bank's working contact. Opens complaints, drafts and submits the bank's reply, uploads documents, and can request a clarification or an extension.
Principal Nodal OfficerSenior contact. Everything a Nodal Officer can do, plus updating who the nodal officer is, approving reassignment requests, and filing an appeal against an Ombudsman decision.
Entity AdministratorSame working rights, plus the administrative ones.
Data separation Every request is checked against the bank the signed-in user belongs to. A user from one bank cannot see or touch another bank's complaints — the system refuses outright rather than returning an empty list, and there is no "unknown bank" fallback that could leak a case.

3. How the progress ladder works

This is a separate indicator from the complaint's own status. It exists to tell the RBI officer whether the bank is actually engaging. Two rules govern it:

  1. The bank never names a status. It performs an action — opening the case, saving a draft, attaching a file, submitting — and the system works out the corresponding rung. A direct attempt to set the progress indicator is refused by design.
  2. It only moves forward. Skipping rungs is fine; going back is impossible. A bank that opens a case and immediately submits a full reply jumps from "Opened" straight to "Reply submitted".

4. Clocks and deadlines

15 daysfor the bank to reply, from the day it is forwarded
3 daysof inactivity before the RBI officer gets a nudge
Every 15 minhow often the system checks for lapsed deadlines
10:00 pmnightly check that formally records a breach
This is the one module where deadlines genuinely bite Unlike the RBI-side modules, the bank's clock is actively monitored. Three separate jobs run: one nudges, one marks cases overdue, and one records the breach that unlocks proceeding without the bank. Officers are notified at each stage.
But a late reply is still accepted There is no hard cut-off. A bank can submit after the deadline and the system records it, noting that it arrived after the window closed. The consequence of missing the deadline is that the RBI officer may proceed without the bank — not that the bank's reply form locks.
An officer can set the deadline manually, within limits An RBI officer may set a specific reply date, but the system refuses a date in the past, on a weekend, or on a declared holiday. When the system calculates the date itself it silently rolls forward to the next working day.

5. Things to know before relying on this

What you might assumeWhat the code actually does
"Asking for an extension buys the bank more time" It is recorded and the officer is informed, but it does not move the deadline or pause the clock. Only the officer changing the date does that.
"The bank can offer the complainant a settlement here" There is no settlement-offer feature anywhere in the bank portal. Settlements are recorded by the RBI officer on their own side.
"The reminder threshold can be changed and takes effect immediately" No — the threshold is copied onto the case when it reaches a progress rung. Changing the configuration afterwards does not affect cases already in flight.
"Any forwarded complaint can be replied to" Only if the tracking record was created when it was forwarded. If that record is missing, every attempt to reply, draft or upload fails with "not found" — the complaint is visible but unanswerable.
"The bank can close a complaint once it has replied" It cannot. Submitting a reply hands the case straight back to the RBI officer.
"Uploading several documents saves the valid ones" No — all files are validated first. If one fails, nothing is saved.
Technical appendix — for developers and testers

The ladder is not Complaint.status

entity/ReActivityStatus.java is a separate entity-progress enum, rank-ordered: 0 NOT_OPENED · 1 OPENED · 2 UNDER_REVIEW · 3 RESPONSE_BEING_PREPARED · 4 DOCUMENTS_UPLOADED · 5 RESPONSE_SUBMITTED (terminal) · 6 OVERDUE (terminal, system only). null is read as NOT_OPENED. The RE touches Complaint.status exactly once — → re_responded, timeline RE_RESPONDED, bell notification RE_RESPONSE to complaint.assignedOfficer.

Sole advancement rule

ReActivityStatusService.applyTransition → ReActivityStatus.canAdvanceTo, whose whole body is target.rank > this.rank. PUT /re-portal/activity-status returns 403 re.activity.manual_set_forbidden by design.

Arrival and the tracker row

TriageController → ReResponsivenessService.trackForwarding() creates RE_RESPONSE_TRACKER: forwardedAt = now, windowDays = timeline.re.response_deadline_days (default 15), windowExpiresAt via businessHoursService, breached=false, exParteEligible=false. No tracker row ⇒ every RE write path throws NoSuchElementException ⇒ 404.

Deadline setting

ReResponseDeadlineService.setDeadline writes to both Complaint.reResponseDeadline and NodalOfficerRecord.reResponseDeadline. An officer-chosen past date, weekend or holiday is refused; a computed default is corrected forward to the next business day. respondToComplaint logs "after window expiry" and proceeds — no hard cutoff.
